The whole purpose of this project is to be able to keep a birds-eye view of what I am working on, and know where 
the separations are.

So, for example. Imagine I fork a codebase and import it into this app. Or store the github url to it.

Then I create a sprint. With the sprint comes an intention, what done looks like, what good enough looks like,
what technologies to use, constant (ie what can't be changed), what is flexible and open to change or can be sacrificed, etc.

This forces me to break things down into pieces and into sprints, which will work with both humans and machines.


Then once I have a sprint set up, I create cards for it, and determine which cards depend on others.
For example, maybe one card can't be completed until another has been completed. Maybe I learn in the
middle of the sprint that two or three cards are dependent on one another. Maybe I have 3 cards all complete,
but tell an AI to merge them and has trouble because the approach the ai used to solve the 3 cards is completely
different. (This specific case might require human interference, resolution.)

That's okay. The whole point of this is to break things up into these pieces/sprints and then let the ai (and humans) work on
things, piece-by-piece, card-by-card. And taper them together along the way and eventually merge it all together
into the final state at the end of the sprint.


I am going to make a lot of mistakes along the way and a lot of incorrect assumptions.
So the first main goal here is to play and have fun with things and use it for actual projects, and shape my tools
as I use them.
